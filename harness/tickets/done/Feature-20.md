# Feature-20. Let students apply together before allocation

## Intent

Students may apply for projects together before assignment, using one shared ranked application. Allocation must keep those students together but may add other students. Make the distinction between the original applying group and students added by allocation visible. This is separate from the post-allocation **Formal proposal** (display terminology agreed in [Bug-23](Bug-23.md)).

The owner confirmed: applying groups contain 2–5 students; all members consent; a creator manages the shared ranking; all members approve the current membership and ranking before submission; changes invalidate approvals; departures reopen the application; a departing creator transfers ownership or dissolves the group; administrator pins apply to the whole group and conflicting pins are rejected.

Hold period/catalogue, student population, rankings and pins constant in comparative tests; vary individual versus indivisible shared application units.

Relevant current entry points are `StudentApplicationController`, `ApplicationPeriodService`, `AllocationPinService`, `AllocationService`, `AllocationOptimizer`, and the frontend Applications/Allocation views and API services. Inspect all callers and snapshot readers before editing. Do not implement this as independent member applications that can split during optimization.
- No email/notification service, new dependencies, teacher approval of pre-allocation groups, automatic grouping based on idea ownership, or change to formal-proposal fields/grading. Invitations and membership changes must be visible in the authenticated Applications workspace; external delivery is not required.
- Coordinate with Bug-23 for display terminology and Bug-22 for deterministic testing. Neither ticket's implementation is a functional prerequisite. Do not rewrite done tickets or historical runs. Do not read or edit the human-maintained root backlog.
- Scope-gate allowances: `docs/SPEC.md`, `backend/SPEC.md`, `frontend/SPEC.md`. Existing backend/frontend source and authored preview/tooling paths and development guides are already permitted. Do not add broad allowlists.

### Functional requirements

- **REQ-001 — One effective application per student/period:** Continue supporting individual applications. A submitted shared application represents all accepted members once, replacing their individual applications atomically at successful group submission; never count both. Pending invitations do not replace an application or reserve a student. A student may accept membership in at most one applying group per period; reject competing acceptances atomically. Existing solo applications remain eligible until replaced, and must not be silently restored after group withdrawal, departure, dissolution or reopening. Show clearly whether the student's effective submitted application is individual, shared or absent.
- **REQ-002 — Formation and management:** An authenticated student creates a period-scoped applying group and is its sole creator/manager. Use the display-name-only student lookup to invite other students; no automatic consent or adding teachers/admins, duplicate members, self-invitations, or cross-period membership operations. The creator manages invitations and the shared ranking; other members cannot edit either. Invitees can see the proposed roster/ranking, accept or decline, and accepted non-creator members can leave before the deadline. Allow a creator-only draft while forming the group, but submission requires 2–5 accepted students including the creator; no sixth accepted member. Pending invitations are not accepted members and are ineligible for allocation.
- **REQ-003 — Versioned consent:** Every accepted member, including the creator, explicitly approves the same current roster and ranking revision. Invitation acceptance is not blanket consent to future changes. Submission requires every current member's approval, 2–5 accepted members, and at least three distinct accepted catalogue projects ranked in order. Only the creator edits/submits/withdraws the shared application. Any actual roster or ranking change invalidates all revision approvals and reopens a submitted application as a noneligible draft until everyone approves and the creator resubmits. A declined/pending invitation alone is not a roster change. Reject stale approval/submission requests; consent and validation must be enforced in the service, not just the UI.
- **REQ-004 — Departures, transfer and dissolution:** Before the deadline an accepted non-creator may leave; the remaining application reopens and needs renewed approval. The departing student can apply individually or join another group, with no automatic restoration of an earlier solo application. Fewer than two remaining members means the draft cannot submit. The creator must transfer ownership to another accepted member before leaving, or dissolve the group with explicit confirmation. Ownership transfer preserves one creator, does not itself change the roster/ranking or approved revision, and removes the old creator's management permissions. Dissolution withdraws any shared submission, releases all members and shows each affected member that they must apply again; no stale eligible application remains.
- **REQ-005 — Deadline and concurrency:** Group creation, invitations, acceptance/decline, approvals, ranking/membership changes, transfer, dissolution and submission/withdrawal are allowed only while the period is open and before its deadline. Lock these at closure/finalization just like existing applications. Recheck current roles, catalogue membership, revision, deadline, accepted-member uniqueness and size atomically on writes. Concurrent final-seat acceptances, accepting two groups, editing versus approving/submitting, leaving versus submission, and submission versus deadline closure must never produce partial replacement, duplicate eligibility or unapproved allocation input. Existing individual write endpoints must reject attempts to create/update/withdraw an independently eligible solo application while a submitted shared application represents that student; leaving/withdrawal is explicit, never an implicit side effect of an old client request.
- **REQ-006 — Atomic allocation units:** A submitted applying group is one indivisible allocation unit. All its members are assigned to the same project, or all are explicitly unassigned. Allocation may combine it with solo applicants or another indivisible applying group, provided each unit stays intact and the final project group contains 3–5 students. A pair must be supplemented to at least three or remain unassigned; a five-member group cannot receive additional students. No student appears twice. Preserve the lexicographic objective: maximize assigned student count, then the existing per-student preference satisfaction; each member uses the shared ranking, so a unit's size counts as that many students, not one application. Preserve existing ranked-project eligibility for unpinned allocation. Do not use a greedy shortcut that loses feasible assignments or splits groups.
- **REQ-007 — Group-aware pins:** Pinning any accepted member of a submitted applying group constrains the entire unit to that project; feasible units remain together, infeasible units remain wholly unassigned. Reject conflicting member pins without changing any pin/application/run. Count effective pinned students, not pin rows, toward capacity five, deduplicating multiple same-project member pins. Existing explicit pins remain independently removable; a remaining member pin continues constraining the group. Replacing/deleting pins must recompute constraints atomically. When membership/consent/submission changes, revalidate existing explicit pins: no conflicting effective group target or capacity violation may be introduced. Do not silently delete, move or broaden an explicit pin to conceal a conflict; report it to the administrator without exposing pin metadata to students. Retain unranked/no-application acknowledgement and catalogue-protection rules, evaluated against the shared ranking where applicable. Pins do not make an unapproved draft eligible as a group: indivisibility follows the submitted application captured by that run.
- **REQ-008 — Snapshot and finalization integrity:** Early, automatic initial and replacement runs snapshot the eligible units, exact roster/ranking revision, effective pin constraints and allocation-origin information. Early runs still leave the period open and stay invisible to students; deadline closure still starts exactly one initial run. Later edits/departures/pins do not change old snapshots/results. Finalization persists final membership and applying-unit provenance so subsequent student views and formal proposals use the entire final group, including additions. Existing stored solo applications/runs/groups without new metadata remain readable and retain their behavior; never infer historical “applied together” membership from equal rankings or idea ownership.
- **REQ-009 — Visible distinction and privacy:** On `/applications`, distinguish “Apply individually” and “Apply together”, the roster/creator, pending invitations, accepted membership, current shared ranking, each current approval, submitted/draft/locked state and deadline. Explain that the applying group stays together but allocation may add members to reach 3–5. Show display names and project titles, not UUIDs. In authorized staff application/allocation views, identify shared units and their members without double-counting students. In student preliminary/final assignment views and authorized staff results, visibly group members who applied together and distinguish members added by allocation. When two applying units share one project, show separate original cohorts rather than claiming everyone applied together. Identify the caller's cohort where present; solo additions are not falsely labeled as an original applying group. Historical rows without provenance use an honest unavailable-origin label, not invented attribution.
- **REQ-010 — Authorization and safe projections:** Only the invited student may accept/decline; only an accepted member approves for themself or leaves; only the current creator manages/submits/transfers/dissolves; existing teacher/admin access remains read-only except administrator pins/runs. Service authorization covers guessed IDs and cross-period references. Unrelated students must not read group rosters/rankings/invitations. An invitee receives only the roster/ranking needed for informed consent. Student final-group projections may show authorized group display names and provenance labels but no additional member UUIDs, pin/admin-origin data, private class/subject assignments, other groups' applications, grades or reviews. Never expose invitations, consent, group-application membership/provenance or audit data in anonymous archive metadata, DOM, links or downloads.
- **REQ-011 — Pagination and interaction:** All new collections follow repository pagination metadata/default/maximum rules. Invite lookup and inbox, staff summaries and group/result lists must expose usable pagination or explicitly load all pages; regression coverage must place the relevant invite/student/unit beyond page size 20. Show loading, empty, error/retry and validation states without losing valid input or treating unavailable data as empty. Confirm replacement of individual submissions, leaving, withdrawal and dissolution with their consequences; cancel sends no mutation, preserves current state and returns focus to its trigger. Success refreshes roster/ranking/approvals/effective application and dependent lists; retain focus on the changed item, invite lookup after invite changes, or Applications primary action when leaving/dissolving closes the group view. Errors remain visible and do not display successful consent/submission.
- **REQ-012 — Contract and documentation:** Update the shared/backend/frontend SPEC rules consistently for individual versus shared applications, consent, group-aware pins, immutable provenance and the distinction from Formal proposal. Search every occurrence of applications, allocation, group membership, pins and invariants in all three specs, not just the Applications section. Add minimal developer/preview guidance for supported fixtures and commands introduced by this feature. Backend annotations remain the OpenAPI source; document new request/response fields, requiredness/nullability, array limits (2–5 accepted submission members; at least three distinct preferences), UUID formats, role/lifecycle conflicts and consent-revision rules. Use existing HTTP/error conventions and validate referenced members/projects as well as request shape. Do not hand-edit generated artifacts.

### State-transition and integration rules

- Key data concepts: period-scoped applying group with one creator; invited/accepted members; revision-bound approvals; shared ordered preferences/submission; immutable allocation-unit snapshot; explicit versus effective pins; finalized membership with origin cohorts. These are data requirements, not prescribed new tables or libraries.
- Creation/registration/role-change and old solo/seed paths must not automatically enroll students into applying groups. Validate that current members are students when accepting, submitting and snapshotting; if accounts become ineligible, do not silently allocate part of a shared unit. Produce an explicit invalid-unit outcome or block the invalid submission, following the existing run failure/result conventions, never split the remaining members.
- Group membership and eligibility are scoped by period. Retain the existing no-duplicate-assignment invariant across the school-year allocation paths; inspect old/create/update/withdraw, invitation, transfer, role-change, seed, pin, scheduler, replacement and finalization paths, including concurrent requests. Preserve pre-existing solo data; no bulk conversion.
- Initial draft formation must not destroy existing solo submissions. At shared submission, replacement of every affected individual application and activation of the shared application is one transaction after all consents, with clear prior warning. Once a submitted shared application is reopened or withdrawn, its members have no eligible shared submission until resubmitted; old solo submissions do not reactivate.
- The implementor must record the resulting route/model inventory and request/response/status table in the handoff, citing controller binding, service authorization/domain conditions and shared exception mappings for each status; do not invent blanket error responses unsupported by the operation. Extend existing own-application/period projections and staff summaries so members see one coherent status rather than `NOT_SUBMITTED` beside a submitted shared application.

- **G3 — Indivisible optimization and measurable objective (REQ-006):** Fixed deterministic inputs: pair plus solo becomes three; pair without feasible filler remains wholly unassigned; five-person unit receives no extra member; two units of two and three can share a five-person project; capacity four plus pair never yields six or splits the pair. ## Acceptance criteria (the Evaluator judges)

1. Students can consensually submit one shared 2–5-member application with at least three distinct ranked projects, without duplicate eligibility or unauthorized replacement. Covered by G1/G2/G6.
2. Creator/member permissions, revision approvals, departures, transfer/dissolution and deadline locks follow the confirmed rules atomically, including concurrent requests and no restored solo submissions. Covered by G1/G2/G7.
3. Every run keeps submitted units together or wholly unassigned, may add compatible students, enforces final size 3–5, and attains the existing student-count-then-rank objective on deterministic verified cases. Covered by G3/G5.
4. Pins constrain whole submitted units, reject conflicts/capacity violations without partial changes and preserve acknowledgements, explicit-pin removal semantics and student privacy. Covered by G4/G6.
5. Original applying cohorts versus allocation additions remain visibly distinct after finalization; snapshots/legacy records, solo behavior, formal-proposal membership and private/public boundaries remain correct. Covered by G5/G6/G7.
6. Responsive, paginated, accessible interactions have honest loading/error states and specified success/cancel focus behavior. Covered by G7/G8 and the manual visual checks (visual readability requires screenshots, not DOM assertions alone).
7. Specs, annotations, generated types and supported preview guidance agree; all required checks pass, no ignored output is committed, and no unrelated behavior or dependencies change. Covered by G6/G8, standard checks and scoped baseline review. No check is claimed unless performed.
